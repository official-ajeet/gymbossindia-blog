GST on gym memberships trips up a lot of first-time gym owners — not because the rules are especially complicated, but because nobody explains them in gym-specific terms. Here's the plain version. (This is general information, not tax advice — confirm specifics with your chartered accountant, especially around registration and invoicing for your particular setup.)

## What GST rate applies to gym memberships

Gym and fitness centre services fall under HSN code 999723, and are taxed at **18% GST**. This covers membership fees, general gym access, and group fitness classes. It's a flat rate — there's no reduced slab specifically for gyms, regardless of membership tier.

## When you need to register for GST

Registration becomes mandatory once your gym's annual turnover crosses **₹20 lakhs**. Below that threshold, you're not required to charge or collect GST — but once you cross it, registration and compliant invoicing apply going forward.

## The composition scheme option

If your annual turnover is under ₹50 lakhs, you may be eligible to opt into the GST composition scheme for services, which applies a flat rate instead of standard GST accounting. Whether this actually saves you money or complexity depends on your specific revenue mix (memberships vs. personal training vs. merchandise) — this is exactly the kind of decision worth a 15-minute call with a CA rather than guessing.

## What a GST-compliant membership invoice needs

Every membership transaction — monthly, quarterly, or annual — should generate an invoice that includes:

- Your GSTIN
- The HSN/SAC code (999723 for gym services)
- A clear split between the base membership fee and the GST amount
- CGST + SGST (for members in your own state) or IGST (for inter-state, rare for a physical gym but relevant if you sell online training)

## The mistake that causes the most trouble

The most common issue isn't the rate — it's **inconsistent invoicing**. A gym that sometimes hands out a proper GST invoice and sometimes just writes a cash receipt on a notepad is the one that runs into audit trouble, because there's no consistent paper trail matching what was actually collected. Every transaction, whether it's ₹500 cash for a day pass or a ₹15,000 annual membership, should go through the same invoicing process.
